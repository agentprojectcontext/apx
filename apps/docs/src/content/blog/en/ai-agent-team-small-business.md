A small business doesn't need a dozen AI agents on day one. It needs a first job that saves time without creating a second job to fix mistakes. A weekly research brief is a useful place to start: it has an observable result, can use public information and does not require the agent to contact customers.

## Write the outcome, not a personality

Ask for something you can inspect: “Find five relevant public updates, link each source, explain the possible impact and flag uncertainty.” Set a deadline and say what would make the brief incomplete. Avoid a vague goal like “grow the business” until the agent has a smaller task it can actually finish.

## Give each role a boundary

A researcher can gather sources; a reviewer can check whether the claims follow from them. A separate owner decides what to do with the brief. Those are responsibilities, not proof that the agent has the expertise of a human specialist. Don't connect private customer data or give permission to send messages for this first trial.

In APX, the [agents guide](https://apxagent.app/docs/concepts/agents/) describes project agents with distinct instructions and handoffs. A [project](https://apxagent.app/docs/concepts/projects/) can represent a business, with readable project context alongside the work. The [tasks guide](https://apxagent.app/docs/office/tasks/) explains how a task keeps a goal, progress and results.

## Review the actual work

Open several sources, compare them with the brief, and check dates and missing context. Record corrections in the project guidance only when they are reusable; do not put credentials in instructions. Check that your chosen [permission mode](https://apxagent.app/docs/concepts/autonomy/) matches the job and understand what happens before external actions.

## Expand one step at a time

After a few runs, measure the minutes you spend briefing and reviewing against the time the work previously took. Track factual corrections and missed sources, not just the number of reports produced. If the report is useful, add a recurring schedule or another narrowly scoped job; if it is not, change the task or stop. A scheduled agent still needs your review, and a role is not a guarantee of a correct result.
