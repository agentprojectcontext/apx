Both connect without a public URL or an extra library: APX opens the platform's own socket from your machine. In
direct messages they work like Telegram: you pair yourself with a code, strangers wait for your approval, and the
outbox keeps every reply. Discord can also look after rooms of your server.

## Discord

### Create the bot

<Steps>

1. In the **Discord Developer Portal**, create an application and open **Bot**.

2. Turn on **Message Content Intent**, then **Reset Token** and copy the token.

3. Under **Installation**, add the bot to your account (or to a server you're in), so you can write to it.

4. In APX, open **Settings › Channels**, **Add channel › Discord**. Paste the token in **Bot token**, choose **Who
   answers** and press **Connect bot**.

5. Write to the bot in a direct message. It answers with a code: type it under **This is my Discord** and press
   **Link**.

</Steps>

Your direct messages now go to Home, like on every channel. Other people who write to the bot directly are strangers
and follow the channel's **Strangers** setting.

### Server rooms

Discord rooms are opt-in. A room that isn't listed doesn't exist for APX: it's neither read nor stored. Under **Server
rooms**, add each one by **Room name** and **Room id**, then **Add room**.

<Aside type="tip">
To get a room's id, turn on Developer Mode in Discord (Settings › Advanced), then right-click the channel › Copy
Channel ID.
</Aside>

Each room has a mode:

| Mode | The bot… |
|---|---|
| **When called** | answers when someone @mentions it, replies to it, or uses one of its names as a word. The default. |
| **When useful** | also judges uncalled messages with a cheap model, against what you wrote in **When an answer is worth it**. |
| **Always** | answers every message. |
| **Read only** | never speaks; the room is kept so agents can read it. |

The rest of the section shapes how it behaves there:

- **What calls it**: extra words that count as calling it, besides an @mention or a reply. Comma-separated.
- **Answers only you**: it reads everyone but answers only you.
- **What it knows and can answer**: your notes for that community (what the project is, where the docs are). They
  are public.
- **Rules**: what it must never say or do there. They go last and win over everything else.
- **Limits**: seconds between answers to one person and answers per hour in a room, so it doesn't flood a busy room.
  They don't apply to you.

<Aside type="caution" title="Rooms are public, so their turns are sealed">
In a room the agent answers with no memory and nothing of yours, even when you're the one writing; its only tools are
web search and reading, when you ask (see [Search the web](/channels/overview/#search-the-web)). It reads
the room's latest messages as data and replies to the message it answers, without mentions. If an answer fails, you
get told; nothing is posted.
</Aside>

Every message of a listed room is kept in that room's chat in APX. Agents can read a room (for example, "what did
they say in #general today?") and post in one by its exact name; a near miss only gets a suggestion, since a public
post can't be taken back.

<Aside type="note">
Discord doesn't send again a message that arrived while APX was disconnected, so a direct message sent during that
gap is missed.
</Aside>

## Slack

Slack connects in **Socket Mode**: the app opens a connection from your machine, so no public URL is needed. Only
direct messages to the app reach your agents; the workspace's channels are ignored.

<Steps>

1. At **api.slack.com/apps**, create an app and turn on **Socket Mode**. Copy the app-level token (`xapp-…`).

2. Give the bot the scopes `chat:write`, `im:history`, `users:read` and `files:write`, and subscribe to the
   `message.im` event.

3. In **App Home**, allow messages in the **Messages** tab. Install the app and copy the bot token (`xoxb-…`).

4. In APX, open **Settings › Channels**, **Add channel › Slack**. Paste both tokens (**Bot token** and **App-level
   token**), choose **Who answers** and press **Connect bot**.

5. Write to the app and type its code under **This is my Slack**.

</Steps>

APX stores the two tokens together as one secret. Pairing, strangers, contacts and the outbox work as on the other
channels; as everywhere but Telegram, WhatsApp and Signal, what you ask there goes through your super-agent first, and cards are answered in
the app ([which channels act on your word](/channels/overview/#which-channels-act-on-your-word)).

<Aside type="tip">
Slack the channel (talking to your agents in Slack) is not the same as the **Slack** integration in
**Resources › Integrations**, which gives agents tools to read and post in your workspace. See
[Integrations](/extend/integrations/).
</Aside>
