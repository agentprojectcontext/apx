A contact can be answered in three ways. **Answered** is the default: the agent replies on its own, in a sealed chat.
**Silenced** means their messages are kept and nobody answers. **Supervised** sits between the two. The agent writes
the reply it would send and doesn't send it. You get the draft and decide.

## What happens when they write

1. Their message is kept in their chat, unanswered. Voice notes are transcribed, since the draft needs the words.
2. APX waits for their burst of messages to settle (about 15 seconds), so three quick lines get one draft.
3. The agent drafts one reply with the same sealed prompt their chat always uses: who they are, your rules for them,
   the relation and the chat so far. It has no tools and it sends nothing. The draft uses the agent's own model.
4. You get a notice on your channel (Telegram, WhatsApp…) and in the app's tray. It shows who wrote (with their
   number on WhatsApp), everything they said since they were last answered, and the draft, with three buttons:

| Button | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Send this** | Sends the draft to them in their chat, as the agent's message. The agent reads it next time as its own words. |
| **I'll answer** | Your next message in that chat with APX (within 10 minutes) goes to them exactly as you typed it, as your own words. Send "cancel" to send nothing. |
| **Ignore** | Nothing is sent. They stay supervised, and what they said isn't drafted again. |

If they write again before you decide, a new draft answers everything they said, and the old one can't be sent any
more. When the agent thinks nothing needs an answer (a "thanks"), or no model could answer, the notice says so and
offers only **I'll answer** and **Ignore**. A closing "ok" or "thanks" brings no notice at all.

Quiet hours apply as for every notice: it arrives without sound.

The buttons work on Telegram and in the app, and on WhatsApp and Signal as numbered options. On other channels the notice comes without them, and you decide in the
app ([which channels act on your word](/channels/overview/#which-channels-act-on-your-word)).

<Aside type="note" title="Limits">
At most 10 drafts per contact per hour, since each draft is a model call. Past that their messages are only kept,
and you're told once. A burst of messages cut short by a restart gets its draft with their next message.
</Aside>

## Turn it on

In the panel, open the contact from the channel's screen and press **Supervise**. Silenced and supervised exclude each
other: turning one on turns the other off. A supervised contact shows an eye next to their name.

From the terminal:

```bash
apx contacts supervise "Ana Pérez"     # or the contact's id; unsupervise to go back
apx contacts suggestions               # drafts waiting for you
apx contacts suggestions send <id>     # send one as it is
apx contacts suggestions send <id> Friday at 10 works
apx contacts suggestions drop <id>
```

## From another app (RPC)

The same choices are protocol methods, so any client can show the pending drafts and act on them. They are owner
methods: call them with the core's token or from a paired device, never with an outside app's token.

| Method | Params | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `contacts.set` | `{ id, name, supervised: true }` (`name` is required, as for any change) | The person |
| `contacts.suggestions.list` | `{ state?: "pending" \| "sent" \| "discarded" \| "superseded" \| "all", person?, limit? }` (pending by default) | `{ suggestions }`, newest first |
| `contacts.suggestions.send` | `{ id, text?, author?: "agent" \| "owner" }` | `{ suggestion, delivery }` |
| `contacts.suggestions.discard` | `{ id }` | `{ suggestion }` |

A suggestion has `id`, `person_id`, `thread_id` (their chat), `channel`, `said` (their messages, oldest first),
`text` (the draft; empty when there is none, with `error` saying why), `state`, `created_at` and, once sent,
`message_id` and `decided_at`.

`send` with no `text` sends the draft. With `text` it sends your edited version instead. `author: "owner"` sends
`text` as your own words rather than the agent's. `delivery.state` says how the send went (`sent`, `queued`,
`uncertain` or `failed` with an `error`). Only a pending draft can be sent or discarded. Anything else is refused with
a conflict, so two taps or two apps never send twice. `send` and `discard` are side effects and need an idempotency
key, like every such method.
