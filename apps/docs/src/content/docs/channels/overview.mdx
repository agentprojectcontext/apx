A channel lets you talk to your agents from an app you already use, and lets other people reach them too. You connect
channels in **Manage channels**, under **Channels** in the sidebar: a screen that lists every account with who answers it and what is
waiting; a row opens its chats, and its two buttons its contacts and its settings (the gear). Each account also shows up in the sidebar under
**Channels**, with its contacts and chats; its name at the top switches to another account.

<Shot name="settings-channels" alt="The Channels screen: each messaging account with who answers it, its state and what waits for approval" caption="Channels" />

An account's settings are grouped in parts and saved together with **Save**: **Account** (its name, connection and
sign-in), **Who answers** (who you talk to, where your messages go, who answers your contacts), **Behavior** (a
message while it works, the web, hours, **Only answers you**, **Limits**, and **What it knows and can answer** and **Rules** for
everyone who writes to the account; a room's or a contact's own rules go after and win), **Stickers and emoji** in chat apps, **Groups**, **Spaces** or **Rooms** where the app has them (named as the app names them),
**Contacts** (everyone on the account, and **Add contact**), **Approvals** (what someone new gets, and who waits) and
**Activity** (what went out). A new account opens on **Account**: name it and connect it; the other parts open once it
is connected, and **Create channel** keeps it.

## Available channels

The current channel catalog has **17 connectable types**. This counts channel adapters, not Google integrations,
MCP servers or paired devices. Each type below has setup instructions; several related services share a guide.

| Channel | Setup and important limit |
|---|---|
| Telegram | [Bot setup](/channels/telegram/); direct chats and listed groups. |
| WhatsApp | [QR linking](/channels/whatsapp/); your line or a dedicated line; listed groups. |
| Discord | [Bot and rooms](/channels/discord-slack/); explicitly allow server rooms. |
| Slack | [Socket Mode app](/channels/discord-slack/); direct messages. |
| iMessage | [Mac permissions](/channels/imessage/); runs on the Mac hosting the core. |
| Matrix | [Homeserver and token](/channels/self-hosted/#matrix); unencrypted direct rooms only. |
| Mattermost | [Bot account](/channels/self-hosted/#mattermost); direct messages only. |
| Signal | [signal-cli](/channels/signal/); direct chats, local daemon required. |
| Email | [IMAP/SMTP mailbox](/channels/email/); different from the Gmail integration. |
| Google Chat | [Pub/Sub app](/channels/work-chat/#google-chat); direct chats and listed spaces. |
| Nextcloud Talk | [Bot user](/channels/work-chat/#nextcloud-talk); direct and listed conversations. |
| SMS | [Twilio number](/channels/sms-ntfy/#sms-through-twilio); provider charges apply. |
| ntfy | [Protected topic](/channels/sms-ntfy/#ntfy); one owner, not a contact directory. |

Start in **Channels › New channel**. A second account of the same app (another WhatsApp number, another bot,
another mailbox) is one more **New channel** of that app. Each account is a
channel of its own: its own sign-in, contacts, chats and agents, its own row in the sidebar, and a **Name** you can
change in its settings. iMessage is the exception: it uses the one Messages account of the Mac. A configured connection is not proof that every room, media type or
approval button is supported: follow the service's guide and test both receipt and reply. See [devices](/devices/phone/)
for the Android app and [integrations](/extend/integrations/) for connected services that are not channels.

## Three words: channel, contact, room

- A **channel** is an app APX talks through: Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack, iMessage, Google Chat, SMS and more.
- A **contact** is a person on a channel. Each contact has a direct chat with your agent.
- A **room** is a shared place many people read, such as a channel on your Discord server or a WhatsApp group you
  listed.

Every contact chat and every room is kept as an APX conversation, so you can read it, search it and step in from the
panel. Agents use the same words: they can list channels and contacts, write to a contact, read a room and post in
one by its exact name.

To leave an account's groups aside for a while, turn off **Use groups** (**Use spaces**, **Use rooms**) at the top of that part of its settings:
everything that arrives from them is dropped (nothing kept, nothing answered) and agents cannot post there. Your list
of rooms and their settings stay for when you turn it on again.

With more than one account of an app (two WhatsApp numbers, say), an agent writes to a contact from an account it
answers, and your secretary from yours. When the contact is only on accounts other agents answer, the agent is told to
say which one instead of picking one for you.
An account you start adding is kept once it connects; one left unconnected is gone the next time APX starts. iMessage
is one per Mac.

## Where your messages land

When **you** write to your agent on a channel, your message goes into **Home** by default: one conversation with your
super-agent, whether you write from the panel, the terminal, Telegram or WhatsApp. Pick up on your phone what you
started at your desk. Home shows all of it; **You** in a channel's list shows only that channel's part of it: what you
wrote there and what was answered there. In Home, a message that came in or went out on a chat app carries that
app's small mark beside its time (hover it: **Sent on Telegram**, **Came in on WhatsApp**), so you know it reached your
phone or a contact too, not only the app.

**Who you talk to** picks the agent that answers you on a channel: your super-agent or the head of one of your
projects. With your super-agent, **Your messages** chooses between Home and **Own chat**, a chat of that channel's own. A
project's head always answers you in the channel's own chat: Home is your conversation with the super-agent, so
**Your messages** stays on **Own chat** there. **Who answers your contacts** picks, separately, who answers the
people who write to the channel. Changing either one moves the chats it answers that are already open, with their
history; a chat you handed to another agent stays with that agent, and so do a contact or a room you gave an agent of
their own (**Answers** on their card, below).

A channel you set up before this choice existed keeps working as it did: if its agent was not your secretary and
your messages went to Home, your secretary answers you in Home and that agent answers your contacts.

## Which channels act on your word

Most of these apps cannot prove who wrote: ntfy knows only a topic, IRC a nick, an SMS number or a mail's sender can be
forged. **Telegram**, **WhatsApp** and **Signal** do prove the account a message comes from, so they act on your
word directly. On every other channel, what comes as you first goes to your
super-agent, which checks it: when it is unsure it is you, or the request is risky (sending, paying, deleting, a
setting), it asks you in the app. Its answer then goes back to that channel's agent, which does what was decided and
answers you there.

Confirmations and questions are answered **in the app**: the panel, the desktop or your paired phone. On Telegram
their buttons still work, except for a change to your settings (a contact, a channel, an MCP server…), which you
approve only in the app; on WhatsApp and Signal they come as numbered options you answer with a number. On other
channels a card arrives without buttons and says where to answer it.

## Who gets an answer

APX answers you, and the people you approve. Everyone else is a stranger, and you choose what happens when one
writes (**Strangers** in the channel's settings):

| Option | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Send a code** | They get a pairing code. Nobody answers them until you approve it, from the channel's settings or from your notice. |
| **Ask me** | You get a notice with who wrote and what they said. Say yes (the notice's button, or tell your agent) and their message is answered. |
| **Answer** | They're answered right away as a new contact, in a sealed chat with an hourly limit. |
| **Ignore** | Nobody answers them and they aren't listed. |

You can also approve from the terminal, or just tell your agent ("let her in"). The agent asks for your OK before it
lets anyone in.

<Terminal command="apx pairing">{`482913  telegram  Ana: Hi, is this the store?`}</Terminal>

```bash
apx pairing ok 482913 "Ana"    # approve as a contact
apx pairing no 482913          # drop the request
apx pairing me 482913          # this address is mine (yours, the owner's)
```

## Contacts are sealed

An approved person is a **contact**, with a role: you (owner), contact, or blocked. A contact's chat is **sealed**:
the agent answers them with no tools and none of your memory, so a stranger's message can't make it act for you.

Click the name at the top of a contact's chat to open their card; under the name, small marks show what is set
for them (silenced, supervised, rules of their own, what their chat may do). The card sets:

- **Relation**: client, lead, associate, provider, family, friend, partner, coworker or other. It shapes how the agent
  talks to them; **Not set** clears it.
- **Answers**: **Like the account** (its agent for contacts) or **Another agent**, picked from your super-agent and
  your projects' heads. Their own agent answers them on every account, their open chats move to it, and changing the
  account's agent for contacts leaves them with theirs.
- **Who they are**, in your words. It reaches only the turns that answer this person.
- **Rules**, which go last in the prompt and win over the agent's caution.
- **What this conversation may do**: **Note appointments**, **Take requests** (each becomes a task for you),
  **Answer from my notes** (your general notes, read only) and **Answer at any hour**. Each one follows the account by
  default (**Account: Yes** or **No**), or you set **Yes** or **No** for this person only.

What every contact of an account gets by default is in that account's settings, **Contacts › For your contacts, by
default**: all off until you turn one on. A contact's own choice wins over it, on that account and any other.
- **Silenced** (a switch): their messages are kept but never reach a model. A silenced contact's chat shows a
  crossed-out bell beside its name in the Inbox, and a supervised one an eye.
- **Supervise** (a switch): they aren't answered on their own; you get the answer the agent would send, to send it, edit it or
  drop it. See [Supervised contacts](/channels/supervised/).

A room's or a group's chat has its own card, beside the chat like a contact's (the **Room** or **Group** button at the
top shows and hides it): **Answers** (**Like the account** or **Another agent** for that room only; changing the
account's agent for contacts leaves it with its own), **When it speaks in the room** (when called, when useful, always,
or read only, which is how you silence a room) and the **Rules** for every room of that account, the same ones as in
the account's **Rooms** settings.

The face of the agent that answers shows in front of its name, after anything else on that line ("Associate · Roby
answers"): under each contact's or room's chat in the Inbox and in the channel's list, and at the top of an account's
list. Accounts with rooms or groups have a **Rooms**, **Groups** or **Spaces** button there, beside **Contacts**.

An account goes by the name you gave it. If you left the app's own name (WhatsApp, Telegram), it goes by the name the
app gives it instead (your WhatsApp profile, the bot's name) or by its number, address or bot; its icon says the app.
Small marks show what it does for everyone on it: answers only you, keeps attention hours, has rules, books
appointments, takes requests or answers from your notes. In **Manage channels** what it does not do shows dimmed, so
accounts compare at a glance. Point at a mark to read it. A contact's own marks show at the end of their row.

The account's mark and name at the top of its list are one button: it opens a menu with its rooms, contacts and
settings, then your other accounts. On a phone those tools are only in that menu.

## An account's menu

Right-click an account, in **Channels** or in the sidebar, to open it, edit it, see its contacts, turn it off or on,
**Silence notices** or **Disconnect** it (after asking). With its notices silenced, what happens on it (someone new
writing, a draft waiting, a room past its limit) stays out of your tray and your chat app; failures still arrive, and
everything is still in the account (its approvals, its chats). **Notify me again** turns them back on.

### Search the web

A channel's **Search the web** setting opens one thing, and only that, in its sealed chats (a server room, a
contact's chat): searching the web and reading public pages (`web_search`, `web_read`). Never your computer, the
shell, files, the browser, your memory or your projects.

- **Only you** (the default): when you ask in a room ("Nova, look at the open issues on our repo"), or from the app in
  a contact's chat.
- **Everyone**: anyone it answers there can have it look something up.
- **Off**: it only talks.

In a contact's chat in the panel, **Who answers** switches between the agent (you tell it what to answer) and
**I'll write** (your words go out as you typed them).

```bash
apx contacts                 # people and their addresses
apx contacts add Ana Pérez   # add one by hand (link addresses with pairing)
apx contacts block <id>      # or unblock, rm
```

## While the agent is working

**While it is working** decides what happens if you send another message before the answer is done:

- **Slip in** (the default): your message joins the running turn; the agent reads it between steps and keeps going.
  This works on APX's own engine; other engines wait.
- **Wait**: your new message waits, and is answered when the current one finishes.
- **Interrupt**: it stops what it was doing and starts over with your new message.

Whatever the setting, a short "stop" ("stop", "pará", "basta", "cancelá") while it works stops the turn: you get
"Done, I stopped." and nothing else.

Every message reaches the agent, in whatever language it comes: it decides when an "ok", a thanks or a goodbye
needs no answer and leaves it unanswered. On Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Slack and iMessage, **Emoji reactions**
(on by default) also lets it answer with an emoji on their message instead of words; where the app takes no
reactions (Slack, iMessage) the emoji goes as a message. Turn it off and the agent answers with words or not at all.

## Attention hours

A channel answers contacts at any hour until you turn on **Attention hours** in its settings: the days it answers and
from when to when, in the time zone of your account (**Settings › Account**). Hours that end after midnight belong to
the day they start on (Friday 20:00–02:00 covers the first hours of Saturday).

- Outside them, a contact's message is kept in their chat, unread for you, and no turn runs: nothing goes back. A
  voice note is still transcribed and a sticker named, so the answer later can read them.
- When the hours begin, each chat that waited gets **one** answer to everything that person sent meanwhile. Chats
  are answered a few at a time, the longest waiting first. A supervised contact gets its draft then.
- If they write again once the hours have begun, that message is answered with the earlier ones in view, and only
  once.
- You are always answered, on any channel. A contact with **Answer at any hour** (in their card, or for every contact in the account's settings) is too.
- In rooms, outside the hours only you are answered, and nothing said meanwhile is answered later.

You can also ask your super-agent in chat ("answer WhatsApp Monday to Friday 9 to 18, and my dad at any hour"): it sets
a channel's hours once you approve it, and who is answered at any hour as its mode allows. Other agents cannot.

## Limits

Each answer to a contact costs a model call, so every account caps how many it gives on its own in an hour, under
**Behavior › Limits**:

- **Answers per hour to one contact** (20 unless you change it). Past it, that person's messages are kept in their
  chat unanswered for the rest of the hour, and you hear it once.
- **Answers per hour in all**, for every contact of the account together (0, the default, is no limit). Past it, the
  account's contacts wait the same way, and you hear it once.

You are never limited. Rooms have their own limits, under **Rooms**: seconds between two answers to one person, and
answers per hour in a room.

## Delivery and notices

Everything APX sends goes through a durable **outbox**: a message survives a restart and is retried, and each one ends
as sent, maybe sent or failed. The channel's settings list what was **Recently sent**; the terminal shows more:

<Terminal command="apx channels outbox">{`10-08T09:14  sent      telegram  reply   Your order shipped this morning…
10-08T09:02  sent      telegram  notice  Morning brief: 3 tasks due today…`}</Terminal>

In **Settings › Interruptions**, **Confirmations follow you** sends a question an agent asked in the app to your channel
if nobody answered it (after 1, 2 or 10 minutes, or never). Your super-agent's own questions come whole, with buttons
on Telegram and numbered options on WhatsApp and Signal; elsewhere it reminds you to answer it in the app ([which
channels act on your word](#which-channels-act-on-your-word)). Another agent's question comes as one line from your
super-agent saying who waits and for what ("Fausto is waiting for you: …"), with no buttons, to answer in the app.
Quiet hours and the daily cap live in **Settings › Interruptions**.

## Voice notes

Voice notes you send on Telegram or WhatsApp are transcribed and answered as text. Transcription uses the engines in
**Settings › Voices** (local whisper.cpp included). Ask the agent to answer you with voice and it turns spoken answers
on for that chat; that needs a voice in **Settings › Voices** too.

## Which channels work today

| Channel | State | How it connects |
|---|---|---|
| [Telegram](/channels/telegram/) | Complete | A bot you create with BotFather |
| [WhatsApp](/channels/whatsapp/) | Complete | A linked device, by QR: chats and the groups you list |
| [Discord](/channels/discord-slack/) | Complete | A bot: direct messages and server rooms |
| [Slack](/channels/discord-slack/) | Complete | An app in Socket Mode: direct messages |
| [iMessage](/channels/imessage/) | Unverified | On a Mac: tools agents use, and (optional) answering the people you approve |
| Matrix, Mattermost, Signal | Unverified | In the panel, not yet tested against a real server |
| Email | Unverified | A mailbox over IMAP and SMTP (an app password); tested only against a fake server |
| Google Chat | Unverified | A Chat app over Cloud Pub/Sub (no public address): direct messages and the spaces you list |
| SMS | Unverified | A Twilio number, checked every few seconds (no webhook) |
| ntfy | Unverified | Push notifications to your phone, and your replies from the ntfy app; only you |
| Nextcloud Talk, IRC, Twitch | Unverified | A bot user (Nextcloud), a nick (IRC) or a bot account (Twitch chat); their rooms are listed like Telegram groups |
| Microsoft Teams, LINE | Unverified | A bot (Azure Bot, LINE Messaging API) that delivers to a [public address](#a-public-address-for-teams-and-line) |

The terminal adds Telegram bots only. Every other channel is set up in **Channels**.

## A public address for Teams and LINE

Teams and LINE don't let a bot fetch its messages: they send each one to a web address. APX only listens on your
computer, so these two need a **public address**, set in their screen under **Channels**. It publishes
only a separate door for webhooks (`…/hooks/<channel>`), never the panel or the API, and each message is checked
against the platform's signature before anything reads it.

| How | What you need | The address |
|---|---|---|
| **Tailscale** | Tailscale connected, and Funnel allowed for this device in your tailnet | Fixed: `https://<this computer>.ts.net:8443` |
| **ngrok** | ngrok installed and signed in once (`ngrok config add-authtoken …`) | Usually the same one each time (a free account keeps one); the panel tells you if it changes |
| **My own** | An https address of yours that forwards to the webhook port on this computer | Yours |

Copy **This channel's address** into the platform (Teams' messaging endpoint, LINE's webhook URL). APX remembers the
last address ngrok gave: if a restart brings a different one, the panel says so, and you paste the new one.

<Terminal command="apx channels">{`telegram       telegram  running   @acme_store_bot    agent nova
whatsapp       whatsapp  running   +1 555 0100        agent nova`}</Terminal>
