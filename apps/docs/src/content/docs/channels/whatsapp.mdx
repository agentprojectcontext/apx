APX links to WhatsApp the way WhatsApp Web does: as a linked device of a number, scanned with a QR. It isn't the
WhatsApp Business API, and there's no bot account to create.

## Two ways to use it

**A line of its own** (the default). A second number, used only by your agent. You write to it from your own phone,
like you'd write to an assistant. This is the setup most people want: your agent has its own chats, and yours stay
yours.

**Your own number.** Turn on **This line is my own phone** and you talk to your agent in WhatsApp's
"Message yourself" chat. What you type to other people on your phone stays yours: APX never answers in your name
there.

You can switch later with the same toggle in the channel's settings.

## Link it

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings › Channels**, use **Add channel** and pick **WhatsApp** (or open it directly if it's the first).

2. Choose **Who answers**, and set **This line is my own phone** if you're linking your own number.

3. Press **Link**. A QR shows up; it changes every few seconds and the page keeps it fresh.

4. On the phone that has the number: **WhatsApp › Settings › Linked devices › Link a device**, and point it at the
   code.

</Steps>

The status turns to **Connected**. WhatsApp support loads the first time you link it, so that first connection can
take a moment.

## Link your phone to the line

On a line of its own, APX doesn't know which number is you until you tell it. Until then, what you write to the line
is a stranger's. Two ways, under **Your phone**:

- **Write to the line** from your own WhatsApp. The request shows up in the list; tap **It's my number**. WhatsApp
  shows the number, so no code is needed.
- **Add my number**: type it with the country code (`+1 555 0100`) and press **Save**.

Once linked, your messages go to **Home**, or to a chat of their own if you set **Your messages** to **Own chat**.

On your own number there's nothing to link: whoever writes in "Message yourself" is you.

## Contacts only

APX reads one-to-one chats. Groups, broadcasts and WhatsApp channels are ignored. Voice notes in private chats are
transcribed (see [Voice notes](/channels/overview/#voice-notes)), and options the agent offers you arrive as a
numbered list: answer with the number.

## Strangers

Strangers work as on every channel (**Send a code**, **Ask me**, **Answer**, **Ignore**), with one difference on your
own number: APX never sends a code from it. There, the first option is **List them**: the person is listed in the
channel's settings for you to approve, and nothing is sent to them.

**Ask me** is often the most comfortable on WhatsApp: you get a notice with who wrote, their number and their first
message. Say yes (the button, or tell your agent) and that first message is answered.

## When it disconnects

- If WhatsApp logs the device out (you removed it from **Linked devices**, for example), APX deletes the stored
  session and the channel shows an error. Press **Link again** and scan a new code.
- **Unlink WhatsApp** removes the channel and its session folder.

<Aside type="caution">
APX uses WhatsApp's linked-device protocol through an open-source library, not an official API. Use it for normal
conversations, not bulk messaging.
</Aside>
