## Mailbox or Gmail integration?

An **Email channel** reads incoming mail through IMAP and answers through SMTP. The **Gmail integration** is a
separate connection for agents to search, read, draft and send when you ask. Connecting Calendar does not connect
either one. See [Email and signatures](/office/email/) for Gmail and local sender signatures.

## Connect a mailbox

1. Prefer a mailbox dedicated to the agent. Enable IMAP with the provider and create an **app password** where
   required (for example Gmail, iCloud or Yahoo); an ordinary account password may not work.
2. Open **Channels › New channel › Email**. Enter the address, app password and optional display name.
3. Known providers fill the IMAP and SMTP servers. Otherwise enter each host, port and security mode. Typical secure
   choices are IMAP 993 with SSL/TLS and SMTP 465 with SSL/TLS or 587 with STARTTLS; use your provider's actual settings.
4. Set the sign-in name if it differs from the email address. Connect and check the status before testing.
5. Under **Account › Your account**, type the addresses you write from in **Your addresses** (no code is needed).
   Send a short message from one of them and verify a reply.

**None** security is only for local mail servers, not an internet connection. APX stores the password as a secret;
it does not display it again. Changing server details or password reconnects the mailbox.

## Restrict incoming mail

**Who may write** accepts individual addresses or a domain such as `@example.com`; your own addresses are always
read. An empty list lets anyone reach the stranger handling chosen in **Approvals**; it does not approve everyone.
An email account never mails a code to someone it does not know: with **List them**, they wait in **Approvals** with
what they wrote, and nothing is sent until you approve them. Start with a narrow allowlist and review the
[supervised reply mode](/channels/supervised/).

## Marked mail

Every mail that reaches the inbox, and the server's **Spam** folder, is brought in. A mail a person wrote is handled
as above; any other is kept in its sender's chat with a mark at its foot and is never answered, never told to you and
never given to the agent:

- **Automatic**: no person wrote it (newsletters, auto-replies, bounces, senders such as `security-noreply@`).
- **Spam**: your mail server put it in its Spam folder (found by its Junk flag or a usual name, read about every ten
  minutes or as soon as new mail arrives).
- **Unverified**: its server could not verify the sender (DKIM, SPF or DMARC), while **Read unverified mail** is off.
- **Never read**: its address or domain is on the account's **Never read** list.
- **Outside who may write**: **Who may write** has addresses and this sender is not among them.

The account's list shows people by default; its filter button shows all mail or one mark, and a line at the end says
how many chats of marked mail are hidden. A chat leaves the marked ones for good once its sender writes a mail a
person wrote. Mail sent from the connected mailbox itself is skipped to avoid echo loops, and unverified mail from your
own address is dropped: it is never kept as yours.

Mail from **Your addresses** counts as yours only when your mail server verified it (DKIM, SPF or DMARC), even with
**Read unverified mail** on. Your mail goes to the account's own chat, one mail after another: email has no Home
switch and no **While it is working** choice.

**Read unverified mail** weakens the sender check: enable it only if you understand why your server does not check
senders. A From address is not proof that you authorized an instruction. Incoming email remains outside content.

## Reading received mail

A mail sent with formatting shows in its chat as it was sent: subject, sender, date and the formatted body. Nothing
in it runs, and its pictures stay hidden until you choose **Show pictures** (a remote picture can tell the sender you
opened it). The agent reads only the mail's words. On a sender's contact card, **Never read again** has a switch for everything from its
domain and one for that address (with the whole domain on, the address is covered too); the contact is blocked and its card and chat stay, and turning the
switch off reads them again.

## What you are told

When someone you have not reviewed writes a mail a person wrote, you get an alert only for mail the auxiliary model marks as important
(a person writing to you, money, accounts, deadlines), like a mail app's alert: sender, subject and one short line,
plain, without the body or links. Newsletters, promotions and pitches stay in the account's chats without a notice.
Without an auxiliary model no alert is sent.

## Recover older mail

IMAP can read messages retained in the selected mailbox, but APX starts at its end on the first connection and
then reads new messages by UID. Reconnecting does not import older mail or revisit messages previously filtered out.
Other folders, deleted messages and mail no longer retained by the server are not included.

The owner can explicitly import one page of up to 25 messages with:

```bash
apx channels email-history email
```

Replace `email` with the account ID shown by `apx channels`. The JSON result reports `stored`, `already_seen`,
`skipped`, `failed`, `has_more`, `next_uid` and `uidvalidity`. If `has_more` is true, pass the returned cursor and
mailbox numbering to the next page: `apx channels email-history email NEXT_UID UIDVALIDITY`. Stop on errors;
if the server renumbers the mailbox, restart from the first page. Already recorded messages are not imported twice.
`already_seen` includes live messages still being processed normally. A failed silent import rolls back its display
and context writes and leaves its UID for retry: after fixing the error, repeat that page with a new request. Failed
live messages are reported as failures rather than silently retried, because they may already have caused an answer.

Recovery only examines UIDs the live reader has already passed, so it cannot consume a newly arrived message before
its normal alert or answer. A missing or changed mailbox numbering is refused. Capturing a sender does not change
how their later personal mail follows the account's stranger policy; an explicit contact decision still wins.
An already-open operator receives recovered message updates without an unread-message flood.

Recovery uses a separate read-only connection: it does not mark mail as read, change the live cursor, start agent
turns, send replies or issue importance alerts. Recovered mail keeps the server's arrival date. Existing allowlists,
blocks, stranger-ignore settings and sender verification still apply, so scanned messages are not all necessarily
stored. The protocol method `channels.email_history` supports pages of up to 50 messages; importing history remains
an owner-only action because it brings private old correspondence into APX.

## Replies, signatures and recovery

Replies stay in the mail thread and send from the configured mailbox. Set its local signature in
the mailbox's own settings (see below); this does not change your mail provider's signature. Ask for a draft or
use supervision if every outgoing answer needs review. A sent email cannot simply be taken back.

If nothing arrives, check IMAP access, app password, allowlist and sender verification before disabling safeguards.
If reading works but sending fails, check SMTP separately: the two services can have different ports and policies.
Inspect the channel error and outbox rather than assuming a queued message was delivered.

## Several mailboxes and signatures

Gmail integration and an Email channel are separate connections. Gmail uses Google's API; an Email channel reads
IMAP and sends SMTP with a mailbox's app password. Configuring a signature changes only local APX settings, not
Gmail settings, existing drafts or messages already written.

### Gmail

In **Resources › Integrations**, open Gmail. **Edit connection** changes its connection or sign-in;
**Configure integration** opens the new mail check and the connected account's signature. Which projects use it is
chosen on the integration's own page, under **Where it is used**. An integration with nothing to configure has no
**Configure integration** button.

The signature has two forms: **Formatted** (the HTML, with a preview as you type) and **Plain text** (for mail without
formatting, required while the signature is on). With Gmail connected on your own Google client, **Bring from Gmail**
fills both with the signature Gmail adds; what APX cannot keep (styles, tables, fonts) gives way to plain lines, and
nothing is saved until you save. With APX's Google client Gmail's settings cannot be read: paste the signature instead.
APX never changes Gmail's signature, and sending an existing Gmail draft does not append or replace its signature.

### SMTP mailboxes

Each mailbox is an account of its own in **Channels**: its pencil opens its settings, the name at the top of them
switches to another account, and **New channel › Email** connects another without replacing the first. Each has its own servers, credentials, answering
agent, access preferences and signature. Selecting a mailbox does not change another mailbox's signature.

When writing through an agent, specify the intended email channel if more than one account could reach the person.
Signatures stay stored after disconnection and can be reused when the same Gmail identity or SMTP channel identity
is configured again. They are not copied into the global default.

SMTP delivery is covered by local fake-server tests; this is not verification against a real mail provider.

### Optional default signature

**Settings › Account › Default email signature** is editable even before connecting a mailbox. It can include plain
text and optional basic HTML, with links and hosted HTTPS images. An enabled signature requires plain text; APX
rejects unsupported HTML rather than executing it or silently rewriting it.

APX chooses one signature, never both:

1. A saved account signature overrides the default.
2. Turning automatic signing off for that account and saving explicitly disables both signatures for that account.
3. **Use default signature** removes the account override. APX then uses the enabled global default, if any.
4. Ask to omit a signature for one message to bypass either choice. This does not remove a signature already in the
   message body.

You can also ask your super-agent to configure these settings. It reads the saved value and asks for approval before
changing it. If another edit happens in the meantime, the stale change is rejected. Use **Reload signature** before
editing again; reloading discards unsaved changes in the form.
