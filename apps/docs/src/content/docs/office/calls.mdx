You can **call** any of your agents using speech and transcription services on Windows, Linux or macOS. Choose the
services separately, including remote servers. APX also has its own local voice on supported Apple silicon Macs;
that option's hardware requirements do not apply to calls using other services.

## APX's voice

Open **Settings › Voice and calls**. It has three parts: **Speak** (the voices your agents talk with), **Hear** (what
turns voice notes and dictation into text) and **Calls**. Voice notes and read-aloud messages try the Speak and Hear
lists in order: the first that answers is used. Calls keep their own choices, described below. APX's own voice is the first row of both, **APX — On this computer**,
with its switch, the model it uses and the folder its models are kept in:

- **Female or male** (female by default), the **language** (Spanish or English) and then, for Spanish, the **accent**:
  neutral (Latin America) or Argentine. Press **Try** to hear it. The voice you pick is the one your agents speak
  with, and the one a call starts with. Under the list, **Try** says any text with the first voice that answers, or
  with another one you pick.
- On a Mac with Apple silicon it downloads on its own the first time, from the installer's app too. A Mac with less than 32 GB of memory gets the
  light voice (about 3 GB), which keeps it quick with everything else open; from 16 GB you can pick **Full** under
  **Quality** (about 6 GB), which sounds more natural. You can follow the download there, or turn the voice off.
- It runs with [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/). When this Mac has none, APX downloads it into its own folder, so an
  install from the app's installer needs no terminal.
- To transcribe without APX speaking, **Install** in APX's row under **Hear** downloads only what hears (about 0.6 GB
  for the light voice), without the speaking voice.
- With less than 8 GB of memory, or on another computer, APX's voice does not run: use a voice service of your own
  in the same list (OpenAI, Gemini, QVox or any compatible server), and a transcription service for your voice notes.
  The row says why it can't run.

Below it are your own voice services; **Add** sets up another one, and the arrows change the order. **Transcription**,
further down, works the same way: APX first, then your own. Once it is ready APX's voice is first in both, so your
agents use it unless you move another service above it. The voices are APX's own, made for it: no real person's voice.

### A voice of your own

To use a recording of your own instead of one of APX's voices, put it in the `voices` folder of APX's home with that
voice's name: `es-AR-female.wav`, `es-AR-male.wav`, `es-female.wav`, `es-male.wav`, `en-US-female.wav` or
`en-US-male.wav`. Add a `.json` with the same name and what the recording says (`{"refText": "…"}`): the voice copies
it more faithfully. A clean recording of 10 to 30 seconds works best. Use only a voice you have the right to use.

<Aside>
The voice loads when something needs it and leaves memory after 10 minutes unused. The first voice note or call after a
while takes some seconds more; the call card shows a bar with about how long is left, learned from the last loads on
your Mac.
</Aside>

## Choose services for calls

In **Settings › Voice and calls › Calls**, choose **Your services** under **Voice for calls**. Pick a speech service
and a transcription service from the enabled services above. An explicit choice is independent of their general order:
if you disable or remove it, choose another before calling. **First enabled above** chooses the first enabled service
in that list when the call starts. Each call keeps both services: if one fails, the call ends without sending your audio
to another provider.
**Manage services** takes you back to those lists to add or edit a service.

- **Gemini** uses its speech API. Set an API key, a speech model and a supported voice, such as Kore.
- **ElevenLabs** uses its native speech API. Add your API key and copy the voice ID from the voice's details in your
  ElevenLabs voice library. **Advanced options** lets you change the model and service address.
- **QVox** and **Custom** use an OpenAI-compatible speech endpoint. Enter its URL, model and voice. A server on a PC,
  private network or VPS can be used; the core, not your browser, must be able to reach that address.
- For listening, use an OpenAI-compatible transcription service, a local Whisper server, or whisper.cpp. A speech-only
  provider does not transcribe. Configure whisper.cpp's model through **Hear** if you want local CPU transcription.

On Windows and Linux, install [uv](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/getting-started/installation/) on the core's computer first;
APX currently downloads uv automatically only on supported Macs. Turn taking runs on the core's computer using CPU
models. The first call downloads its Python dependencies and small
voice-activity and end-of-turn models with uv; it does not download MLX or Qwen speech models. Calls need an available
microphone and browser audio permission. Audio sent to a remote speech or transcription service is handled by that
service. Responses are played after each sentence's synthesis completes; provider audio is not token-streamed. Live
partial transcription is unavailable in service mode. Interruption stops playback and cancels pending requests, but a
provider may continue processing or billing a request it has already accepted.

Select **APX's voice** instead to use the local Apple silicon option and its voice/accent controls.

## Make a call

Calls are on unless you turn them off: **Settings › Voice and calls › Calls › Allow calls**. With calls off the phone buttons are hidden,
and your agents still speak and hear voice notes.

Press the phone button at the top of the panel to call your super-agent, or **Call** on any agent's profile, its menu
or its chat. With APX's local voice, pick the voice and accent; APX remembers them. Other services use the voice saved
in their service settings. It rings while the voice loads, then the voice answers
with a greeting and listens: what you say before it answers is not heard. Speak normally: you can interrupt the voice
at any moment, and saying "ok, I'll wait" leaves it quiet until there is news.

What you say shows in the call's chat (with services, after each spoken segment), and the voice's words as it says
them. You can also type in the
call's chat while it goes on: the voice takes it as said and answers aloud. The call card moves anywhere by its top
and folds to it, so it never covers what you need. A click on its top, without dragging it, opens the call's chat
again, wherever you went meanwhile.

When you ask for something that needs real work (what is in a project, a mail, your calendar, something on the web),
the voice says it is on it and the agent you called does it while you keep talking. Ask for several things at once and
each one runs on its own; you hear each answer as soon as it is ready, never over your own words.

To end, say goodbye ("chau", "bye", "I have to go"): the voice says goodbye back and hangs up. **Hang up** ends it at any
time.

## Wake by voice

Say "ok" or "che" followed by your super-agent's name, and a short voice request opens with no hands, even with the
phone locked or the app closed. The voice answers "¿Sí?", you ask, it answers in one short sentence and hangs up on its
own, unless it needs to ask you something.

- **On your Android phone.** In the APX app, open **Settings** and turn on **Despertar por voz**. It is per phone.
  While it is on the phone listens in the background: a notification says so and Android shows the microphone in use.
  The phone only picks out speech by loudness and sends short clips (under 3 seconds) to your computer, which transcribes
  them with its own voice (APX's voice or whisper.cpp) and checks for the name. Nothing is kept.
- **The panel on the screen.** When the name is heard, a small floating panel appears over whatever you are doing, or
  over the lock screen, with a hang-up button. Expand it for a phone-call screen: your agent's picture at the top, waves
  that follow its voice, and the hang-up button. The same screen opens when you call from the app's call button.
- **On your computer (optional).** In **Settings › Voice and calls › Wake by voice**, choose the phrases (they are the
  same for every device; empty means "ok", "che" or "hey" plus the name) and turn on **Listen on this computer** for
  this computer only.
- **Hang up by voice.** In any call, "cortá", "listo, nada más", "eso es todo", "hang up" or "that's all" ends it, and
  the voice can end it itself. You can also hang up from the phone or, when the call is on the phone, from the live-call
  bar in the panel.
- **A ready greeting.** The greeting is recorded ahead in your voice and language. On Android, the phone waits for
  your core to accept the call before playing it or opening the call's microphone, while the voice finishes loading.
  Wait for the greeting before speaking. When you change the voice or its language, the greeting is recorded again;
  Settings says so while it is being made.
- **Stopping on Android.** Turning off listening cancels pending requests triggered by the name, but does not hang up
  a manual call. Hanging up or unpairing cancels pending call starts too; a late response cannot reopen the microphone.
  A request already sent may still reach the core, but the phone ignores its response after cancellation.
- **A greeting with your chosen service.** If calls use a remote speech service, preparing the greeting sends its text
  (including your first name, when set) to that service before a call. Turning calls off cancels pending preparation
  and stops new recordings. Reading voice settings alone never records a greeting.
- **In the chat.** The call's chat shows it as a quick voice request and the phone it came from.

The call's voice, model and services are the ones set on your computer: the phone only carries the audio.

<Aside type="caution">
To open over other apps, Android needs the **Display over other apps** permission for APX; the app asks when you turn
the switch on. Some phones also stop background apps to save battery: if listening stops by itself, exclude APX from
battery optimization in Android's settings.
</Aside>

## Where calls go

Each call is one chat in the **Calls** group of the Inbox, with what you said and what the voice answered. Each thing it
asked the agent hangs inside the call as a card: open it to see how it was done. Calling again from a call's chat goes
on in that same chat.

## The model a call talks with

A call talks with **your main model**, the one APX uses by default, so there is nothing to set up. To use another one,
pick it in **Settings › Voice and calls › Calls › Model for calls**: any model APX has, including a server of your own added as a
provider. A small, fast model makes the conversation feel quicker. The agent you call always works with its own model.

## Settings in `core.env`

| Variable | Default | What it sets |
|---|---|---|
| `APX_VOICE_AUTO` | `on` for the default home | `on` or `off`: download APX's voice on its own where this Mac runs it. A core with its own `APX_HOME` doesn't, unless this says `on`. |
