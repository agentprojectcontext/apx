An **artifact** is what an agent makes for you to look at or use: one page of HTML that APX shows in a safe frame.
It comes in two kinds:

| Kind | What it is | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Widget** | A chart, a table, a status card, a form, a small tool. The default. | In the chat, on a project's [Board](/office/board/), on your phone. |
| **App** | A full window with its own navigation, often with records you add over time. | Fullscreen, listed under **Apps**. |

Agents make a plain widget unless you ask for an app or the thing must keep records (a log, a list of clients, an
inventory). A widget made to show you something during a chat is a simple page, nothing more.

Every artifact belongs to one project (or is global when it was made outside one), keeps its name across versions,
and updates live wherever it is open. Both kinds are built with **APX Kit**, APX's own components and theme, which
APX adds to the page wherever it shows: no downloads, nothing outside your machine.

## What an artifact remembers

Each artifact has its own memory, kept on your APX across openings, reloads and new versions. Nothing is shared
between artifacts.

| Layer | Holds | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| **State** | What you chose or typed: filters, the open tab, a checklist, a draft. Up to 64,000 characters. | You, and its agent, who reads it as data and can change it for you. |
| **Store** | Data it cached, such as the last reading of a live widget. Up to 5 MB. | The page. Its agent sees only the names of what is stored and the size. |
| **Database** | The records an app keeps (SQLite), up to 50 MB. | The page. Its agent sees the tables and how many rows. |

To see or clear it, open the artifact's menu › **Memory and export**: each layer shows its size, and **Clear…** lets
you choose what to forget. Copying an artifact to another project does not copy its memory.

### Credentials

An artifact that reads an API with a key never holds the key. You save it once as a secret named
`artifact.<artifact name>.<key>` (for example `artifact.weather.token`), and the page asks APX to make the call: APX
adds the key on its side and the page only gets the answer. The call can only go to the sites the artifact declared,
and the key is never shown to the page, to its agent or in a shared link.

## Live artifacts

How an artifact behaves when you leave and come back:

- **Static** (most of them): it shows what it was made with, and runs only while on screen.
- **Cached**: it keeps its last reading in its store, so when you open it again you see that reading at once (marked
  as stale) while it fetches a fresh one.
- **Live**: a page that must keep running while hidden (a ticker, a timer) can ask APX to keep it alive. APX keeps at
  most three such pages running at once, pauses them when the app window is hidden, and stops the least recent when a
  fourth opens.

## Export and import

Menu › **Memory and export** › **Export** saves the artifact as one file, `<name>.apx-artifact.json`. By default it
carries the page and a short description of it, **without** its memory; turn on **Include memory** to take your state,
store and database with it (for a backup or a move to another computer). **Import artifact** on a project installs
such a file there as a new artifact; if the name is taken it gets a number.

<Aside type="caution">
An export that includes memory carries what you typed and the app's records. Share only exports made without memory.
</Aside>

## Bigger apps

Most apps are one page an agent writes by hand. For a large one, an agent can build it from source (with Preact and
Vite) into the same single page and show it with the terminal:

```bash
apx artifacts dir expenses --project <project-id>     # where its source lives
apx artifacts push expenses dist/index.html --app --project <project-id>
apx artifacts list
```

The source lives in APX's folder, under `artifact-sources/<project>/<name>`, never in your project's own files. Your
records stay in the app's database, so rebuilding it never loses them.

## Marketplace (coming)

Exported artifacts are the format a future marketplace will use. Each file holds:

- a **manifest**: name, title, kind (widget or app), version, the kit version it uses, the sites it reaches, the
  secrets it expects (names only, never values), whether it runs live, and the version of its database layout so a
  newer version can update your records;
- the **page** itself;
- optionally, the memory (state, store and database), only when you export it for yourself.

An upload to the marketplace will never carry your memory: whoever installs an artifact starts with an empty one and
adds their own secrets.
