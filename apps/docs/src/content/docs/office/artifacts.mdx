An **artifact** is what an agent makes for you to look at or use: one page of HTML that APX shows in a safe frame.
It comes in three kinds, each with its own color and icon in lists:

| Kind | What it is | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Widget** (violet) | A chart, a table, a status card, a form, a small tool. The default. | In the chat, on a project's [Board](/office/board/), on your phone. |
| **App** (green) | A full window with its own navigation, often with records you add over time. | Fullscreen, listed under **Apps**. |
| **Web page** (blue, a globe) | A whole page, a landing or a site, as any browser shows it. | In the chat like an HTML block, and on a screen of its own. |

Agents make a plain widget unless you ask for an app or the thing must keep records (a log, a list of clients, an
inventory). A widget made to show you something during a chat is a simple page, nothing more.

Every artifact belongs to one project (or is global when it was made outside one), keeps its name across versions,
and updates live wherever it is open. Widgets and apps are built with **APX Kit**, APX's own components and theme, which
APX adds to the page wherever it shows: no downloads, nothing outside your machine. A widget's cards look like the cards agents draw in their answers, so a widget and an answer read alike.

## Web pages

Ask an agent for a web page ("make me a landing for…", "a page I can edit") and it shows in the chat the way an HTML
block does: **HTML** and its title, **Preview** or **Code**, **Copy** (its HTML), **Expand**, **Edit**, and under
**More** sharing its link, a window of its own and **Download (.html)**. A web page has its own styles on a light
page, not APX's look, keeps no memory and reaches nothing outside its frame; its own small scripts (a menu that opens)
run.

**Expand** opens it on a screen of its own, where it fills the space and scrolls like in a browser. **Desktop** and
**Phone** lay it out at a computer's or a phone's width, and **Code** shows its HTML. **Edit** turns on edit mode
there: the whole page at that width, scrolling on its own, with the edit panel fixed beside it (under it on a phone),
so a long page or a whole site is as easy to change at the bottom as at the top. When something you ask needs the
agent, you are taken back to the page's chat with the request in the message box. An agent can turn a widget into a
page, or back, when you ask.

## Pointing at what to change

The pencil on a widget in the chat, or next to it in the **Widget** side panel, turns on edit mode, the same as
[editing a page in the browser](/office/browser/#editing-a-page-an-agent-made): click a part, change its text, colors,
font size or spacing and see it right away. Click another part to edit that one; **Your changes** keeps only the parts
you changed.

What you change there is the page itself: **Save changes** writes it into the widget as a new version, with no agent
in between. What you write in **Anything else?** (a change of design, a new section) goes to the agent instead:
**Save and ask Vera** saves the rest and puts that request in the message box for you to send. A part the widget's own
code draws when it runs is not in its page, so that change goes to the agent too. Discard, or Esc, leaves the widget as
it was.

The widget you edit runs apart from the panel, in a page of APX's own browser with no internet and none of your
logins, so it reads what it remembers but changes nothing. Editing a widget needs Chrome, Chromium or Edge on the
computer where APX runs.

## What an artifact remembers

Each artifact has its own memory, kept on your APX across openings, reloads and new versions. Nothing is shared
between artifacts.

| Layer | Holds | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| **State** | What you chose or typed: filters, the open tab, a checklist, a draft. Up to 64,000 characters. | You, and its agent, who reads it as data and can change it for you. |
| **Store** | Data it cached, such as the last reading of a live widget. Up to 5 MB. | The page. Its agent sees only the names of what is stored and the size. |
| **Database** | The records an app keeps (SQLite), up to 50 MB. | The page. Its agent sees the tables and how many rows. |

To see or clear it, open the artifact's menu › **Memory and export**: each layer shows its size, and **Clear…** lets
you choose what to forget. Copying an artifact to another project does not copy its memory.

### Credentials

An artifact that reads an API with a key never holds the key. You save it once as a secret named
`artifact.<artifact name>.<key>` (for example `artifact.weather.token`), and the page asks APX to make the call: APX
adds the key on its side and the page only gets the answer. The call can only go to the sites the artifact declared,
and the key is never shown to the page, to its agent or in a shared link.

## Live artifacts

How an artifact behaves when you leave and come back:

- **Static** (most of them): it shows what it was made with, and runs only while on screen.
- **Cached**: it keeps its last reading in its store, so when you open it again you see that reading at once (marked
  as stale) while it fetches a fresh one.
- **Live**: a page that must keep running while hidden (a ticker, a timer) can ask APX to keep it alive. APX keeps at
  most three such pages running at once. Pages should pause unnecessary work when hidden; a hidden page can expire
  or be evicted when another needs a slot. See [Live apps](/apps/live-apps/) for the separate six-app pool and limits.

**Apps stay open; widgets close.** An app you open keeps running while you go elsewhere in APX, like an app on your
desktop: come back and it is exactly as you left it, until you close it (Close app) or quit APX. Up to six run at
once, each marked with a dot in the sidebar's Apps. A widget is a view: open it full screen, look, leave, and it
closes. Pin a widget to Apps to make it an app. "Open in a new window" moves an app to a window of its own; the
panel shows where it went and brings it back when that window closes.

## Export and import

An artifact travels as one **`.apxart`** file, widget or app alike. Right-click it in Apps (or its More menu) ›
**Download (.apxart)** › **The artifact alone**, or **With what it remembers** to take your choices, cache and records
too (for a backup or a move to another APX). **Install artifact** (the upload button in Apps, or a project board's +)
opens a dialog: drop the file or choose it, see what it is (widget or app, what it connects to, which secrets it will
ask for, whether it brings data), pick the project, pin it to Apps or not, and only then **Install**. If the name is
taken there, it installs beside it with a number. From a terminal: `apx artifacts export <name> [--memory]` and
`apx artifacts import <file.apxart>`. Files saved earlier as `.apx-artifact.json` install the same way.

<Aside type="caution">
An export that includes memory carries what you typed and the app's records. Share only exports made without memory.
</Aside>

## Publish a public copy

Opening `artifact.apxagent.app` without a copy code takes you to the APX website. Links with a copy code open that published copy.

An artifact's **Share** asks who can see it, each answer with its own link to copy:

- **Only you**: in your APX and on your devices. **Copy private link** opens it there.
- **Public**: an independent copy at `artifact.apxagent.app` that anyone with its link sees without an account.
- **Live from this computer**: the artifact live from your own running APX, only while this computer is on and has
  a public address (Settings › Devices).

Choosing **Public** asks you to sign in to your [APX account](/start/account/) first when you are not, with
**Continue with Google**, and carries on by itself once your browser is done. Then one step shows the copy as anyone
will see it and its size. The widget's saved data stays out unless you switch on **Include the data this widget
saved**; **See which data** and **See the code** show exactly what goes. Only **Share public** uploads it, and copies
its link. The Share panel then shows the link with **Copy public link** and **Update with your changes…**; choosing
**Only you** again asks **Stop sharing?** first. Opening the page does not use the account service: exhausting that
service's quota can stop publishing, but does not by itself stop existing links from opening. Public hosting has its
own limits. An agent's request also needs this step and your confirmation, even when it can otherwise work without
asking. On your phone, open APX on your computer to review that request; the phone cannot approve a public copy
without the review.

The HTML itself can contain sensitive information. Review it too: omitting state cannot remove information
already written into the page. A public copy never includes the artifact's store, database or secret references,
and has no connection to APX. Its sandbox and content policy block ordinary requests and external resource loads;
features that require those connections will not work there. This is not anonymous browsing: browser mechanisms
such as WebRTC/STUN or DNS prefetch may still reveal your IP address to another service.

**Settings › Account › Published copies** shows each copy as a card with its link, **Copy link**,
**Update with your changes…** (from the artifact it came from, or one you choose when this computer did not
publish it) and **Stop sharing…**. Choosing **Only you** in the artifact's Share panel also stops sharing its copy,
after the same review. Updates keep the same link and always
start with no state selected; local edits are not uploaded automatically. If the original artifact is gone,
choose another local artifact for an update. Stopping publication does not need the original and does not
delete it; it makes future reads of the link fail and notifies connected pages to remove the preview.
A page hidden in the background checks again when you return. It cannot recall files someone already downloaded.

On a public page, **Open in APX** opens the existing installation dialog in the desktop app. It does not
install automatically or need the recipient to sign in. **Download file** saves an `.apxart` that you can open
or drag into APX instead. The copied state can be excluded again before installing.

There is a limit of 20 copies per account, each up to **10 MB (10,000,000 bytes)**, including HTML and selected state. Creating an artifact keeps it local: only an explicit publish action and your confirmation upload a copy. Referenced videos and other files are not fetched or embedded. If your chosen content is too large, the entire copy is refused before upload; your local artifact stays in APX. Live viewer connections are limited to
20 per copy and 100 per account; if those connections are full, wait for another viewer to close the page and retry. A changed artifact, changed account or
expired review requires a fresh review before publishing. Free cloud quotas also apply: if unavailable,
your local artifacts still work. Canceling a review you started releases that local draft; it does not publish
anything. Closing an agent's review leaves its waiting request available to reopen. Completed copies do not
use the local review allowance, and stopping publication has separate review capacity. Pending review data is
kept privately in your APX data folder; back up the whole folder, not just its database.

## Bigger apps

Most apps are one page an agent writes by hand. For a large one, an agent can build it from source (with Preact and
Vite) into the same single page and show it with the terminal:

```bash
apx artifacts dir expenses --project <project-id>     # where its source lives
apx artifacts push expenses dist/index.html --app --project <project-id>
apx artifacts list
```

The source lives in APX's folder, under `artifact-sources/<project>/<name>`, never in your project's own files. Your
records stay in the app's database, so rebuilding it never loses them.

## Marketplace (coming)

Exported artifacts are the format a future marketplace will use. Each file holds:

- a **manifest**: name, title, kind (widget or app), version, the kit version it uses, the sites it reaches, the
  secrets it expects (names only, never values), whether it runs live, and the version of its database layout so a
  newer version can update your records;
- the **page** itself;
- optionally, the memory (state, store and database), only when you export it for yourself.

An upload to the marketplace will never carry your memory: whoever installs an artifact starts with an empty one and
adds their own secrets.
