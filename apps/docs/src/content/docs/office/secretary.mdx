Home, your super-agent, can be more than a chat that answers. With the **secretary** profile it becomes a chief of
staff: it opens and closes your day, keeps your commitments ahead and watches your work every few hours, saying only
what is worth an interruption. Everything it (or any agent) says unasked goes through one gate you control.

## Review and improvements

<Aside type="note">Planned for APX 2.14; not included in 2.13 releases.</Aside>

In **Settings › Review and improvements**, decide whether APX reviews new activity and suggests small improvements
as task proposals. It is enabled daily by default. Choose **Weekly**, turn **Review activity** off, or add optional
**Extra details** to focus the review. Details supplement APX's maintained instructions; they cannot authorize actions.
You can also ask your super-agent to change these settings. Turning review off keeps your details.

Reviews may consume credits or plan usage on your auxiliary model. The first check is due after 24 hours; weekly
means seven elapsed days, not a fixed local time. Changing the frequency or re-enabling starts a fresh interval and
activity window. No new eligible activity means no model request, task or notice. No useful finding means silence.

Each check selects eligible completed turns since the previous check, capped at seven days, newest first and
independent of pinned chat order or newer automated turns. The model sample is limited to 24 owner excerpts and
20,000 source characters, plus short relevant project context and open task summaries. Private contacts, rooms, delegated chats, external-client sessions, routines and
recognizable sensitive content are excluded. This is not a complete audit or a guarantee that every secret is detected.

A check samples at most three project scopes (including conversations without a project). Each scope has its own
model request containing only that scope's excerpts, project context and tasks; other scopes in the bounded sample
are skipped. There are no tools, a shared 60-second deadline and at most 2,400 requested output tokens across all
requests. Each scope can produce one proposal, for up to three unassigned proposals with exact evidence, acceptance criteria and limitations, visible in **Tasks** and **For you**.
Nothing is applied automatically. Accepting a proposal is a separate decision; implementation still follows normal
permissions. Duplicate titles are suppressed, but differently worded proposals may still need your judgment.

Review is a native background check, not an editable routine. Existing review routines stay unchanged; turn them off
separately if you no longer want them. A restart does not replay a consumed window. Failed or interrupted analysis
is not retried for that window, to avoid duplicate spending or proposals. Without an auxiliary model, review waits;
configure one in **Settings › Memory** if you want to use it.

## The "For you" tray

**For you** in the top bar is the one place where everything that wants you lands:

| Kind | Example |
|---|---|
| **Needs your OK** | Tomás wants to push the `v2.4.0` tag and asked first. |
| **Task waiting for you** | Lucía's task is blocked on your answer. |
| **Due** | The Northwind quote is due today. |
| **Routine** | The low-stock routine found three products to reorder. |
| **Agent** | Vera finished the photos and let you know. |
| **Channel** | Someone new wrote on WhatsApp. |

An entry leaves by itself once it is answered, done or moved, and a newer notice about the same thing replaces the
unread one. Channels only mirror the tray; the tray is the source.

<Terminal command="apx notifications">{`• 08/10/2026, 09:02:11  needs your OK · Run git push origin v2.4.0 — Tomás wants to tag the rates page release
• 08/10/2026, 08:30:04  routine · Morning brief
  07/10/2026, 18:30:02  task waiting · Send the wholesale quote

2 unread`}</Terminal>

`apx notifications read` marks them all read.

## The interruption budget

Messages you did not ask for (routine news, reminders you set, agents' notices) go through one gate in
**Settings › Interruptions**. Answers to your own messages never count, and neither do confirmations.

<Shot name="settings-interruptions" alt="Settings, Interruptions: unasked messages per day, quiet hours, gaps per project and per kind, critical switches, and the log of what APX meant to send" caption="Settings › Interruptions" />

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Chat app for notices** | The app and your paired phones always get them. Pick one of the channel accounts your super-agent answers to get them there too, or **None** for the app only (an account a project's head answers is that head's line, never a notice line). By default, the first of them that knows you. A question another agent raised arrives there as one line, "Fausto is waiting for you: …", answered in the app. |
| **Unasked messages per day** | The daily cap, 3 by default. Past it, the rest stays in the tray. `0` = no cap. |
| **Quiet hours** | What APX would say unasked waits until they end, then arrives together in one message. Answers still arrive, silently. They follow the time zone in your profile (this computer's when you set none), as does the day the cap counts. |
| **Gap per project** | Two notices about the same project closer than this (180 min): the second stays in the tray. |
| **Gap per kind** | So one repeating condition cannot fill the day alone (120 min). |
| **Something critical may go past the cap** | On by default. It is always recorded and marked. |
| **Something critical may wake you in quiet hours** | A separate permission, off by default. |

Anchor routines (a morning brief) and the reminders you set never count against the cap. Below the settings, **What
it meant to send** lists every unasked message and what happened to it: **Sent**, **Sent together**, **Waiting for
quiet hours to end**, **Only in the tray**.

<Aside type="tip">
A question an agent asked in the app that nobody answered follows you to your channel: your super-agent's own come
whole with their options, another agent's as one line saying who waits, to answer in the app. Set when in
**Settings › Interruptions** under **Confirmations follow you** (after 2 minutes by default).
</Aside>

## Change settings from chat

Ask Home to adjust contact rules, channel attention hours, your time zone, interruption preferences, project
settings or MCP configuration. It can read the available settings and propose changes through `apx_settings`.
It changes them only when you ask it yourself, and its mode says whether it asks first: on Total it just does it.
MCP servers, channels, email signatures and the few other changes listed in
[Autonomy](/concepts/autonomy/) show the before/after fields and wait for your OK in the app, **even in Total
mode**. Reading settings or asking for an already-saved value never asks. Other project agents do not
inherit this settings tool.

Sign-ins, saving secret values, model choices and autonomy permissions remain yours in Settings; do not paste
keys into chat. A variable reference can name an MCP secret you already saved. Settings confirmations are approved
in the panel, desktop or paired phone, not by an untrusted incoming channel message. See
[Channels](/channels/overview/#which-channels-act-on-your-word).

An agent can also offer a concrete next action for you to accept or decline. Accepting expresses your choice;
it does not pre-approve every tool call that carrying it out might require. Normal action permissions still apply.

## The secretary profile

**Settings › Super-agent** says what your super-agent is for. **None** disables profile routines; independent secretary capabilities keep their own switches. **Secretary** gives it the chief-of-staff role, scheduled assistance and the settings it reads. APX also ships a
**Focus coach** profile, and you can **Install profile** from a folder with an `apx-profile.json`.

<Shot name="settings-profile" alt="Settings, Super-agent: where and how often it writes to you, its secretary capabilities and, below, its profile" caption="Settings › Super-agent" />

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Open the day** | Weekdays 08:30 | Tasks and commitments due, today's calendar and the one thing to start with. Always arrives. |
| **Close the day** | Weekdays 18:30 | What moved, what is stuck, what carries over, and whether today's commitments were kept. Always arrives. |
| **Watch every** | 2 hours | Reads your work for something worth an interruption; stays quiet when there is nothing. Goes through the budget. |
| **Team news every** | 4 hours | What your other agents' routines and notices had for you, told in one message. Stays quiet when there is nothing; not counted in the daily cap. |
| **Calendar command** | (empty) | Prints today's agenda (for example `gcalcli agenda`). Its output reaches the morning brief as data. With Google Calendar connected you can leave it empty: the brief reads today's events itself. |
| **A task waiting on you** | 48 hours | After this long it is worth a word. |
| **Raise a commitment** | 48 hours | This long before its date, while there is still time to keep it. |
| **A quiet project** | 7 days | Open work and no movement for this many days. |
| **How it addresses you** | Neutral | Informal, Neutral or Formal. |

**Settings → Super-agent → Secretary capabilities** has a switch for **Team news**, plus **Customize instructions**. It is not an action in New routine. It reuses the internal scheduler: an existing record, prompt and history stay in place, without running or sending anything when the settings screen opens. Enabling schedules the next check; saving or changing a profile does not reactivate a disabled capability.

New mail is checked from Gmail's own settings: **Configure integration** on Gmail's page in **Resources › Integrations** turns it on, sets how often and whether only important mail (see [Integrations](/extend/integrations/)). It needs Gmail connected with your own Google OAuth client; SMTP alone cannot read a mailbox. Mail that came reaches the super-agent as data, never instructions: answering or forwarding still requires your OK. When none of it needs you, it says nothing and no message reaches your chat app.

Day open, day close and the watcher remain ordinary [routines](/office/routines/). The existing profile timing settings still set the check intervals. Customized prompts are preserved.

A morning with the secretary on, in Telegram:

> **Home:** Morning. Due today: the Northwind quote (you owe it to Marta). Tomás's rates page is in review since
> yesterday. Calendar: supplier call at 11. Start with the quote; it's the one with a person waiting.

## 👍/👎 on unasked messages

Routine news and agents' notices arrive as plain messages, with no buttons. To rate one, react to it: 👍 (or ❤️, 🔥,
👏) is useful, 👎 is not, and taking the reaction off takes the rating back. The Interruptions log rates them too, as
**Useful** / **Not useful**.

- Mark a kind 👎 twice in 14 days, with no 👍 after, and the gate drops it ("you marked this kind unhelpful") until a
  👍 lifts it.
- A routine is told its own counts in its next run, so it can adjust what it says.
- Anchors, reminders you set and critical messages are never muted.

## Agents reach you only through notify_owner

Any agent can tell you something unasked, but only one way: its `notify_owner` tool. The message goes through the
same budget as routines, counted per agent (so your 👍/👎 tune each agent separately), and lands in the tray either
way. The tool tells the agent what the gate did, so a dropped message is not retried. It is not available in a
routine's own run (its answer is already delivered), between agents, or in a contact's chat. An agent that writes
to you with `message_contact` (to "me") when you did not ask for it goes through the same budget: past the cap it is
dropped, and in quiet hours its text waits for the end of them. If you chose to be told only in the app, it stays
in the tray and reaches no chat app.

An unasked message reads like the next line of your chat. Before an agent writes one (a routine's news, a task's
report, what is new after an update), it is told how long ago you last wrote and when the last visible reply was
saved. This timing does not confirm delivery or reading, and no text from another chat is shared.
If you talked in the last few hours it just goes on, with no hello and no introduction; only after about six hours of
silence, or on a new day, does it open with a short greeting.

## One voice: the team news

While a **Team news** routine is on (the secretary's is), your other agents never write to you on their own: what their
routines and `notify_owner` have for you waits, and every few hours your super-agent tells it in one message, a line
or two per agent, leaving out what doesn't matter. Only three things skip the wait: the super-agent's own messages, a
routine you made an anchor, and a critical notice. With no team news routine, each agent's news goes through the budget
on its own, as above.

## Commitments at day close

The day close asks how today's commitments went. You answer in your own words ("the quote went out, the photos
slipped to Monday") and the agent records it: **Kept**, **Missed**, released, or a **New date**. Agents never settle a
commitment on their own. See [Tasks › Commitments](/office/tasks/#commitments).
